The Problem

Operational Reality

Produced water is a cost center that steam demand could offset.

Mature and heavy-oil fields generate large volumes of hypersaline brine. Disposal is expensive. Thermal recovery needs continuous steam. Today these run as two separate cost lines.

High
Disposal cost per m³ in hypersaline fields
Continuous
Steam demand in thermal-recovery operations
Unlinked
These two cost centers rarely share infrastructure today
02

The Technology

HOSS™-VWP

One modular system, three recovered outputs.

A closed-loop indirect-heating system that converts difficult produced water into usable field resources rather than waste.

Output 01
Steam
Demineralized, high-vapour-fraction steam for field use or EOR injection.
Output 02
Clean Water
Recovered water suitable for reinjection or controlled discharge.
Output 03
Mineral Solids
Concentrated dried solids for disposal — or feed-specific recovery assessment.
Process

Five stages, indirect heating throughout.

1
Feed Conditioning
Incoming brine is prepared for thermal treatment.
2
Slurry Handling
Controlled management of precipitating salts to limit scaling.
3
Vapour Separation
Steam is extracted and routed for field reuse.
4
Indirect Heating
Waste heat, gas, geothermal, solar thermal, or hybrid input drives the process.
5
Water & Solids Recovery
Clean water is separated; remaining minerals are dried and concentrated.

Risk & What's Unverified

Technical Assessment

Credible design, undisclosed performance.

Strengths

  • Modular — scalable field-by-field, no CAPEX concentration
  • Indirect heating — lower corrosion, simpler operability
  • Closed loop — minimal discharge, regulatory friendly
  • Energy flexible — waste heat reduces fuel exposure

Undisclosed

  • Energy intensity — no kWh/m³ figure published
  • Steam spec — vapour fraction, purity not stated
  • Fouling behavior — no operating/maintenance data
  • Availability — no MTBF or uptime target
Portfolio View

What could go wrong, what could pay off.

Risk

  • Performance falls short of claims, eroding ROI
  • Scaling degrades heat transfer over time
  • Vendor maturity — no performance guarantees seen
  • Energy cost volatility if not waste-heat powered

Opportunity

  • Immediate disposal-cost avoidance
  • Steam self-sufficiency for thermal recovery
  • Modular rollout spreads capital risk
  • ESG-aligned partnership narrative with Oxy

Written performance guarantees tied to liquidated damages — not currently offered.
